Who owns a business flow that crosses five teams?

Last week we talked about Conway's Law in QA: the architecture changed, ownership didn't. In one flow 28/28 contract tests passed, yet a seam between 4 workers stayed unowned.

Here's the question I keep asking on critical journeys:

**Can you name the owner of the seam in under ten seconds?**

Not the module. The seam - the contract between two teams, the event that has to arrive on time, the failure mode when the vendor is slow, the version skew between platform and extension.

If the answer takes longer than ten seconds, the defect is already scheduled. It just hasn't shipped yet.

The most expensive bug you ever shipped lived on a boundary. Between two teams. Between two systems.

Between the platform and the customer's implementation. The modules were healthy. The boundary was not.

This Thursday I'll publish the full breakdown: three boundary layers (technical, organizational, external) and the checklist we use to make the seam visible before it ships. It builds directly on the system from Article 21.

For now - one question for your team:

**Pick one critical flow this week. Who can name the owner of the seam? Comment with the flow - I will reply with a one-line invariant.**

Curious what you find.
