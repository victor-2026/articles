A product can become modular long before its quality model does.

Teams split. APIs multiply. Integrations become critical. Partners build extensions. Releases become increasingly independent.

Yet QA may still be organized as if one team owns one application.

That creates a dangerous gap:

- teams own local quality;
- nobody owns the boundary;
- the business flow fails end to end.

Conway's Law explains why this happens. Architecture reflects communication structures. If team boundaries and quality ownership do not evolve with the architecture, integration risk becomes everyone's problem - and therefore nobody's clear responsibility.

The answer is not to centralize all testing.

It is to make ownership, risks and verification explicit:

- capability teams own the quality of what they build;
- a business-flow owner is accountable for the outcome;
- the central QE function provides standards, enablement and systemic risk governance.

**Tests follow architectural boundaries. Accountability follows business outcomes.**
