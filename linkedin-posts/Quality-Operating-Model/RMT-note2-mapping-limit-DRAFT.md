# The Limit We Didn't Publish: Numbers, Not Gate Shapes

Our profiles encode numbers, not gate shapes. That is the honest limitation of our own method — and until this note, it lived only in a draft. Unpublished honesty doesn't count, so here it is, with its table.

Their B2 gate is an AND of cap and floor: at most one survivor at any N, score above sixty. Absolute cap, no matter the sample size. Ours is a band: five percent at N of twenty and above, with a small-N floor of max one below it. Same letter B2, different machinery — one counts heads, the other measures rates with a guardrail for tiny samples.

Why the difference matters: an absolute cap at N equals ten means something entirely different from the same cap at N equals ten thousand. A band says so out loud; a cap stays silent about scale. Neither is wrong in its home context — but silently swapping one for the other is how a gate passes what it should stop.

[SCREENSHOT: Mapping Limit table — B2 Cap / B2 Score / Decision logic, QAEverest vs VerdictGate]

We checked: this limit never reached our published joint piece. It sat in the draft while readers assumed the gates were interchangeable. That was our miss, corrected here. A method that names where it stops is a method you can place; a method that hides its edges asks for trust it hasn't earned.

Don't copy our five percent — one gate is depth, not breadth. Copy the habit instead: write the limit down, publish it next to the gate, and let skeptics attack the real boundary instead of an imagined one. Same rule as everywhere in this series: machines count, humans decide.
